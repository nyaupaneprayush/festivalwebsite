Welcome to Nepali Festivals

This website celebrates Nepal’s biggest and most loved festivals: Dashain, Tihar, and Chhath. Each of these festivals represents unity, joy, and devotion in Nepali culture.

Dashain Festival

Dashain is the grandest festival in Nepal, symbolizing the victory of good over evil. It honors Goddess Durga, who defeated the demon Mahishasur. Families gather to receive tika and jamara from elders and enjoy delicious food.

People fly kites, play cards, buy new clothes, and celebrate with joy throughout the country. It’s a time for love, blessings, and family reunions.

Tihar Festival

Tihar, also known as Deepawali, is the festival of lights and the second biggest festival in Nepal. It lasts five days, honoring animals like crows, dogs, and cows, and ends with Bhai Tika – celebrating the bond between brothers and sisters.

Homes are decorated with diyos, candles, and lights. People sing Deusi-Bhailo and create colorful rangolis, filling the streets with joy and brightness.

Chhath Festival

Chhath Puja is dedicated to the Sun God (Surya) and Chhathi Maiya. It is mainly celebrated in Nepal’s Terai region. Devotees fast, stand in rivers, and offer prayers to the setting and rising sun for health and prosperity.

This festival teaches purity, discipline, and deep gratitude to nature for giving life and energy to the world.

My Festival Experience

This year, I celebrated Dashain and Tihar with my family. We played cards, decorated our home with lights, and received blessings from elders. The joy and love among family members made it unforgettable.

During Chhath, I visited Janakpur. Thousands of devotees were offering prayers by the river. The sunset and devotion created a peaceful and spiritual atmosphere I’ll always remember.
